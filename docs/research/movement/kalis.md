# Kalis — Thrusting Blade: Movement Research PRD

## 1. Purpose and scope

This document defines the evidence boundary and movement hypotheses for
Hukbo's `WeaponId.Kalis`. It covers both valid implemented loadouts:

- Kalis + light organic armor + no shield; and
- Kalis + light organic armor + Tall Hardwood Shield.

The target is movement behavior, not a reconstruction of a lost fighting
system. It covers formation placement, approach, spacing, engagement,
attack commitment, recovery, disengagement, regrouping, pursuit, and
tactical retreat at individual and contingent scales.

The live source currently defaults to V2, whose roster includes solo and
shielded Kalis. The approved planning target will switch that default to the
already-registered V3 roster, which contains solo Kalis only, without changing
either preset. Shielded Kalis remains available through an explicitly selected
V2 scenario.

Evidence labels have their repository meanings:

- **Documented:** directly described or pictured in a sixteenth-century
  source.
- **Documented, form uncertain:** the class, name, or broad affordance is
  attested, but the exact depicted-period form is not.
- **Provisional reconstruction:** a reasoned movement or gameplay hypothesis,
  not a recovered historical technique.

## 2. Executive finding

**Provisional reconstruction:** The repository's broader weapons synthesis
reports a 1521 blade-name lead elsewhere rendered *calis*, but this movement
research has not independently identified the transcription, edition, or
folio. Until that locator is supplied, it is a period-name lead pending
verification—not a period-attested identity, blade form, or movement system.

**Documented, form uncertain:** Later Philippine museum objects establish a
family of one-handed, double-edged, sword-length kalis or kris forms. A
Cleveland Museum example from Mindanao is 70.2 cm overall with a 56.9 cm blade;
a Metropolitan Museum Tawi-Tawi example is 67.9 cm without its sheath and
830.6 g. Both are later than Hukbo's period and may anchor scale and handling
questions, not a 1500s silhouette or technique.

**Documented, form uncertain:** A current National Museum interpretation
describes long-bladed kris-class weapons as single-handed slash-and-thrust
weapons whose free hand can carry a wood-and-abaca shield. Applying that
interpretation directly to the sixteenth century is
**Provisional reconstruction**. Pigafetta's Mactan narrative separately
describes fighters moving side to side while advancing under thin wooden
shields. These facts support mobility while carrying a shield as a broad
equipment relationship; they do not define directional defense.

**Provisional reconstruction:** Hukbo should make solo Kalis the roster's
measure-and-angle one-handed blade: mobile enough to adjust line, long enough
to discourage a direct rush, and unwilling to stay crowded when outnumbered.
Kalis + Tall Hardwood should preserve the same range awareness while trading
lateral and reverse mobility for a steadier shielded approach. Neither variant
is evidence for triangular footwork, a named guard, a formal duel system, or
unchanged continuity from a modern FMA curriculum.

### 3.2 Mechanical interpretation

**Provisional reconstruction:** A one-handed sword-length blade leaves the
torso freer to rotate than a two-handed weapon and permits either an empty
off-hand or a shield. A point-capable blade can threaten along a relatively
direct line, so a solo Kalis user need not close as deeply as an Itak user.
That supports:

- preserving a narrow buffer outside commitment distance;
- small lateral adjustments to change the line of approach;
- short forward commitment followed by a reset rather than uninterrupted
  forward pressure;
- disengagement before multiple enemies occupy different approach angles; and
- avoiding crowding a teammate's weapon side.

These are object-affordance hypotheses. No period source records Kalis stance
width, preferred lead foot, step sequence, turn rate, attack cadence, or
recovery time.

### 3.3 Modern practitioner comparison

The cited Kalis Ilustrisimo organization page describes forward, backward, and
sideward walking, changes of line, baiting commitment, and direct
counterattack. Its Repeticion Orihinal page advises against edge-to-edge
contact and favors redirecting contact that immediately opens a counter.

**Provisional reconstruction:** This modern or living doctrine is useful as a
falsifiable handling analogy: a live-edged one-handed blade makes distance,
line, and recovery matter. It is not proof that a sixteenth-century Visayan
or Mindanao fighter used Ilustrisimo's named
methods, hourglass pattern, training sequence, or duel conventions.

## 4. Full movement lifecycle

All behaviors below are **Provisional reconstruction**.

### 4.1 Formation placement

- Solo Kalis should begin inside its contingent's irregular frontage, with
  enough personal space to move the blade and change line.
- Shielded Kalis may begin in a roomier existing slot, but never in a rigid
  front rank, shield wall, or fixed bodyguard ring.
- Placement should preserve a route back toward the contingent leader or a
  local ally cluster.
- Mixed battles may place Kalis near other loadouts without requiring mixed
  persistent contingents in the first implementation.

### 4.2 Approach

- Solo Kalis advances toward a locally viable target while keeping the nearest
  second threat from entering on an unobserved side.
- Shielded Kalis uses shorter, more deliberate steps and prefers a distinct
  direct or shallow-oblique approach lane.
- Both variants slow before commitment distance; neither receives a speed
  bonus from numerical superiority.
- A contingent's global posture may permit advance, hold, or tactical
  withdrawal. The individual still chooses its line only from locally
  perceived agents.

### 4.3 Spacing and engagement

- Solo Kalis prefers the outer part of its own effective hand-weapon distance.
  It yields ground rather than letting a shorter Itak user begin at crowded
  contact for free.
- Shielded Kalis uses its own preferred spacing and needs more room and time to
  reverse or turn; the movement model grants no defensive coverage.
- Against a longer Kampilan, Kalis cannot safely interpret its own reach as
  control of the exchange; it seeks a side angle or waits for commitment.
- Against multiple threats, preserving one escape lane takes precedence over
  a prolonged paired exchange.

### 4.4 Commitment, recovery, and guard reset

- Commitment is a short, movement-limited interval: the user may continue
  forward only modestly and may not instantly reverse at full speed.
- Recovery restores locomotion over a small number of ticks; solo Kalis
  recovers lateral choice sooner than shielded Kalis.
- Facing affects locomotion only. It must not change attack arcs, hit
  resolution, damage, shield interception, or hit-location weighting.
- A reset may include a small step or turn, but it is not a named historical
  guard animation.

### 4.5 Disengagement, regrouping, pursuit, and retreat

- Solo Kalis disengages early when local enemies can approach from separated
  angles, then seeks a nearby ally or the contingent's regrouping direction.
- Shielded Kalis yields ground more slowly under mild disadvantage but must
  disengage when opponents occupy multiple approach bearings or encircle it.
- Pursuit is limited when it would break local support, expose the rear to a
  second enemy, or cross the contingent's posture.
- Tactical retreat means yielding, seeking allies, or refusing poor contact.
  It never means panic, surrender, rout, or a morale system.

## 5. Six-loadout 1v1 movement hypotheses

One-versus-one is a temporary local geometry inside a group battle. The table
does not assert formal historical dueling or predict equal win rates. Every row
is **Provisional reconstruction**.

| Opposing loadout | Solo Kalis movement | Kalis + Tall Hardwood movement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kampilan, no shield | Stay outside the first committed cut, use shallow lateral change, then close only during recovery; disengage rather than trade. | Keep one approach lane, advance in short increments, avoid a large turn during commitment, and do not chase past support. |
| Wasay, no shield | Deny a direct planted entry, change line after the axe commits, and reset before the next heavy action. | Accept a narrower approach lane, avoid being fixed in place, and step back after a failed close. |
| Kalis, no shield | Maintain equal measure, avoid endless circling, and use local ally/enemy context to decide whether to commit or leave. | Close deliberately through a collision-safe lane while denying a free circling route. |
| Kalis + Tall Hardwood | Avoid repeating a blocked direct entry; use greater lateral freedom to seek another lane or disengage. | Mirror case: small changes of line, short commitments, and rapid reassessment; no artificial deadlock rule. |
| Itak, no shield | Preserve reach, retreat on the Itak's rush, and turn only enough to keep one line; pursue cautiously after separation. | Deny an uncontested rush through spacing, but maintain room for the shorter blade's crowded-contact pressure. |
| Itak + Tall Hardwood | Refuse a frontal crowding contest; use the solo Kalis's mobility to change line or wait for ally pressure. | Keep slightly wider measure than the shielded Itak and avoid being pulled into a tight turning contest. |

## 6. Count, composition, and group behavior

### 6.1 Knowledge boundary

- Individual Kalis movement reads only perceived nearby allies, enemies, and
  their complete loadouts.
- Contingent or faction posture may use surviving battlefield totals and
  composition.
- Global information may change broad aggression, cohesion, and withdrawal;
  it must not reveal a distant enemy's position to an individual.
- Exact ties, zero-friendly cases, and threshold equality require one stable,
  documented deterministic outcome.

### 6.2 Count scenarios

All rules are **Provisional reconstruction** and should become ratios plus a
small number of stable thresholds rather than bespoke code per scenario.

| Local context | Solo Kalis | Kalis + Tall Hardwood |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1v1 | Hold preferred measure; commit after line or timing improves. | Deliberate shielded close; preserve turning room. |
| 2v2 | Avoid sharing the same approach lane; one Kalis may hold attention while the other changes line. | Provide stable shielded local pressure without forming a permanent wall; the partner must retain an exit. |
| 1v2 | Disengage before the enemies separate into two approach angles; move toward support. | Yield through a free lane; shield presence is not protection from encirclement. |
| 2v3 | Maintain mutual support and refuse isolated pursuit; disengage if one ally becomes screened off. | Anchor the withdrawal direction while the partner avoids being pinned beside the shield. |
| 3v5 | Follow contingent withdrawal or regroup posture; preserve one lane and avoid individual heroics. | Support the local retreat only while an escape lane remains; no last-stand bonus. |
| Local advantage | Shorten decision delay and constrain escape lanes through positioning, never through extra speed. | Advance cautiously enough that allies can use separate lanes. |
| Large battle | Use contingent cohesion and local composition; avoid scanning or reacting to exact remote positions. | Serve as a deliberate local participant, not a rigid front-rank assignment. |

### 6.3 Composition effects

- Nearby Kampilan or Wasay allies need swing room; Kalis should not crowd their
  approach lane.
- Nearby solo Itak benefits when Kalis holds a target at measure, but this is
  emergent cooperation, not a scripted combination.
- Nearby shielded allies reduce the need for Kalis to be the local anchor, but
  do not confer a speed or defense aura.
- A local enemy majority of shielded loadouts should make solo Kalis less
  willing to enter frontally and more willing to regroup.

never modify the factor. “Reach” means the loadout's configured gameplay

| Pursuit support limit | 1.0–1.5 local ally spacing radii | 1.25 | 0.75–1.25 | 1.00 |

Candidate turning policy:

- a small change up to one-eighth turn may retain the listed movement factor;
- a change between one-eighth and one-quarter turn uses a reduced step; and
- a change beyond one-quarter turn should require more than one tick.

The exact representation belongs in the implementation plan. It must be
integer/fixed-point, authoritative, and versioned. No candidate may be copied
into code until deterministic threshold tests and matchup calibration exist.
The shared Tall Hardwood research table is the canonical shield-delta
envelope; the shielded column above is its once-authored, rounded absolute
materialization for Kalis and is never multiplied again at runtime.

## 8. Calibration questions and acceptance evidence

1. Does solo Kalis preserve measure against Itak without kiting indefinitely?
2. Does it still find a viable entry against Kampilan and Wasay recovery?
3. Does shielded Kalis remain deliberate but mobile, rather than immovable?
4. At 1v2, does each variant attempt a readable tactical disengagement before
   encirclement without gaining speed?
5. In 2v2, do two Kalis users select separate usable lanes rather than stack?
6. Do shielded and solo Kalis each contribute in at least one individual and
   one group context without requiring equal win rates?
7. Do exact count-ratio boundaries produce identical results regardless of
   incidental collection order?
8. Across 100v100 and 250v250 mixed battles, do local-count decisions remain
   bounded and avoid adding another independent all-agents scan per factor?
9. Does the new movement preserve old movement-preset replay fixtures when the
   new preset is not selected?

Calibration should record approach distance, time in commitment/recovery,
disengagement attempts and success, isolation time, ally spacing, blocked
movement, state transitions, outcomes, event hashes, and state hashes. Manual
spectator review is separate from automated determinism evidence.

## 10. Unknowns and non-goals

Unknown:

- exact sixteenth-century Kalis blade length, mass, balance, curvature, and
  distribution by region;
- whether a particular form was normally paired with a shield;
- stance, handedness, lead foot, turn rate, preferred measure, cadence,
  feinting, combinations, or recovery;
- 1v1, 2v2, or mass-battle doctrine specific to Kalis;
- historical count thresholds or movement-speed differences; and
- continuity between any named modern FMA method and 1500s practice.

This PRD does not authorize:

- damage, reach, cooldown, combo, clash, shield-interception, or hit-location
  rebalance;
- directional shield defense or attack arcs;
- rigid formations, shield walls, formal duel rules, morale, panic, rout, or
  surrender;
- terrain, fatigue, wounds, pathfinding, projectiles, campaign state, or
  default-roster changes; or
- presenting the later wavy Mindanao kalis as the universal or uniquely
  correct sixteenth-century Philippine form.

The defensible product claim is narrow: Kalis movement is a transparent,
deterministic gameplay reconstruction informed by a period-name lead pending
verification, later one-handed weapon forms, period evidence for mobile
shield use, and carefully bounded modern handling analogies.
