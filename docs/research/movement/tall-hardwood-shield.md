# Tall Hardwood Shield — Movement Research PRD

Research date: 2026-07-29

Status: evidence-led research requirements; no implementation authorization

Parent contract: [`README.md`](README.md)

## Purpose and scope

This document defines the research basis for movement by a warrior carrying
Hukbo's implemented `ShieldId.TallHardwood`, including its layered interaction
with:

- Kalis — Thrusting Blade; and
- Itak — Work Blade.

It covers formation placement, approach, spacing, locomotion-facing,
attack-linked commitment and recovery, tactical disengagement, regrouping,
pursuit, and retreat. It does not authorize changes to attack cadence, reach,
damage, hit location, shield coverage, or interception. Facing is a movement
constraint and readable posture only; it must not produce directional defense.

## Headline finding

The evidence supports a shield as an active partner to movement more strongly
than it supports one exact `TallHardwood` object.

- **Documented:** At Mactan in 1521, Pigafetta describes shields made of thin
  wood and islanders moving from side to side to avoid fire while continuing
  to approach.
- **Documented, form uncertain:** The late-sixteenth-century Boxer Codex
  depicts a tall curved shield carried by a Cagayan warrior. A still image
  cannot establish construction, mass, grip, step pattern, or combat sequence.
- **Provisional reconstruction:** Cole's 1913 Bagobo account documents later
  Philippine practice in which a shield user presents
  the body sideways, crouching, moving forward and backward, seeking an
  opening, and glancing missiles away. His 1922 Tinguian account describes a
  light shield, three-finger grip, and active tilting for deflection.
- **Provisional reconstruction:** A large off-hand shield plausibly imposes
  modest turning, acceleration, visibility, clearance, and recovery costs
  while enabling a deliberate approach.
- **Unknown or unsupported:** No located source supplies historical shield
  speed, turn rate, preferred distance, group interval, local-count rule,
  shield wall, or separate Kalis/Itak shield footwork; therefore the document
  treats every such behavior as a testable gameplay hypothesis.

The safest model is therefore a shared shield movement layer plus two modest
weapon-role specializations, all numerically provisional.

## Identity and terminology boundary

`TallHardwood` is a stable code identity, not a historical conclusion. The
verified corpus contains two different anchors:

1. Mactan thin-wood shields in 1521;
2. the tall curved Cagayan shield identified in the Boxer Codex catalog,
   c. 1590–1595, with exact folio still unresolved.

Legazpi-era breast-high shields and a reported Morga full-body light-wood
shield remain unverified leads excluded from claims until exact
volume/document/page locators are recorded.

Later ethnographies add long narrow, round, oblong, and pronged shields with
different grips and construction. They cannot be collapsed into one object.

Until direct early vocabulary evidence is verified, retain `Tall Hardwood
Shield`; do not state that the Mactan shield was tall or hardwood, apply a
pronged regional form across the archipelago, or name any group geometry a
historical shield wall.

## Source criticism

Pigafetta describes an amphibious attack: forty-nine Europeans crossed shallow
water toward a much larger defending force. Missile exchange, exposed legs,
shoreline distance, and retreat dominate the account. The safe inference is
shield-accompanied evasive approach in that encounter. It does not establish a
universal dodge, melee shield interception, or formation.

The Boxer Codex supports a late-century silhouette and equipment pairing.
Romero's study of its pictorial sources requires caution: the image is not
technical photography and cannot recover hidden construction or motion.

Cole's Bagobo and Tinguian accounts are mechanically useful Philippine
observations, but three to four centuries later and from distinct communities
under American colonial ethnography. They can generate movement hypotheses,
not prove continuity.

Warming's controlled round-shield trials and modern load-carriage research
provide experimental methods only. Their objects, participants, and contexts
are not Philippine tall shields.

## Shared shield movement layer

The shield should express **protected deliberation at a modest mobility cost**,
not a slow tank stereotype.

Compared with the paired weapon without a shield, a bearer should plausibly:

- retain nearly the same unobstructed travel speed;
- accelerate, reverse, and make large turns slightly less freely;
- seek one principal threat bearing and preserve an exit;
- need additional space beside allies;
- use lateral or backward movement to preserve workable geometry;
- commit only through a free lane;
- recover toward allies or open space rather than remain surrounded; and
- disengage tactically as independent threat bearings multiply.

| Lifecycle phase | Candidate behavior | Evidence status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Placement | Use irregular frontage or oblique offset with shield/weapon clearance; avoid exact side-locking | **Provisional reconstruction** |
| Approach | Advance with smaller heading corrections; reposition laterally around blocked lanes | Broad direction **Documented** at Mactan; exact rule **Provisional reconstruction** |
| Engagement | Prefer one principal threat bearing, useful weapon distance, and a visible exit | **Provisional reconstruction** |
| Commitment | Permit an attack-linked step but reduce immediate redirection | **Provisional reconstruction**; gameplay abstraction |
| Recovery | Restore facing, separation, and a free lane before recommitting | **Provisional reconstruction** |
| Refusal | Briefly decline entry if an ally blocks the lane or a second threat can arrive during recovery | **Provisional reconstruction**; gameplay abstraction |
| Disengagement | Yield backward or obliquely toward support/open space | Later comparison only; **Provisional reconstruction** |
| Regroup | Rejoin a perceived ally or contingent anchor without exact alignment | **Provisional reconstruction** |
| Pursuit/retreat | Pursue only while cohesion and clearance remain; withdraw without synchronized army-wide pivot | **Provisional reconstruction**; gameplay abstraction |

## Counts, composition, and group movement

Counts alter willingness and route choice, never physical speed.

| Local condition | Candidate response |
| --- | --- |
| One enemy | Establish one principal bearing, preserve an exit, and commit through a free lane |
| Two separated enemies | Narrow their bearing spread where possible; refuse commitment if the second can enter during recovery |
| Three or more enemies | Prioritize tactical disengagement, regrouping, and reduced pursuit |
| Local advantage | Advance deliberately if distinct ally lanes remain; do not crowd the target |
| Contested | Hold useful distance and protect recovery space |
| Local disadvantage | Yield toward perceived support and refuse poor commitment |
| Severe disadvantage | Disengagement and regrouping dominate unless already committed |

Composition breaks ties but does not turn a weapon into extra headcount.

- With allied Kampilan or Wasay, use an oblique support position and stay
  outside the ally's broad commitment clearance.
- With allied shieldless Kalis or Itak, leave a separate approach and exit
  lane; never trap the ally between shield and opponent.
- With another shield bearer, preserve independent weapon lanes and irregular
  offset; separate when both choose the same lane.
- Against Kampilan or Wasay, respect the opponent's commitment clearance and
  keep lateral options; the shield does not guarantee safety.
- Against Kalis, manage direct approach lanes and rapid re-entry.
- Against Itak, deny an uncontested close entry without backing into allies.

This produces contingent cohesion and emergent frontage, not a shield wall,
shared defensive surface, or fixed rank.

## Kalis + shield specialization

**Provisional reconstruction:** the more point-oriented, lane-conscious shield
pairing.

Candidate movement:

- slightly greater preferred separation;
- controlled direct or shallow-oblique approach;
- narrower weapon clearance;
- smaller lateral corrections while a direct lane remains open;
- recovery by restoring separation and threat-facing posture; and
- support through a distinct threatened lane, not a fixed rank.

Role viability requires individual value in controlling a direct engagement
lane and group value in holding or exploiting a narrow lane while preserving
ally spacing. It need not have equal duel outcomes.

## Itak + shield specialization

**Provisional reconstruction:** the more compact, close-range,
repositioning-oriented shield pairing.

Candidate movement:

- somewhat closer preferred separation;
- more lateral and oblique repositioning before commitment;
- slightly quicker reorientation within the shared shield penalty;
- earlier break-off when a second threat enters local support range;
- avoidance of prolonged direct pressure against longer weapons; and
- use of constrained space or ally-created openings without gaining defense
  from proximity.

Role viability requires individual value in close repositioning and survival,
plus group value in constrained space or transient openings.

No source establishes either specialization as historical technique.

## Matchup and scale requirements

### 1v1 and 2v2

Cover both shielded loadouts against all six loadouts, including mirrors. In
2v2 include homogeneous shield pairs, the mixed shield pair, each shielded
loadout with every shieldless ally, and shielded pairs against Kampilan/Wasay.

Record sustained distance, turn demand, lane conflicts, isolation, commitment
and recovery, disengagement, accidental wall-like geometry, and behavior after
one ally dies. One-versus-one is a temporary battlefield geometry, not a
formal duel.

### Asymmetric and larger groups

Use 1v2, 2v3, 3v5, curated 4v4/5v5/8v8, default 100v100, and stress 250v250.
Measure threat-bearing spread, ally obstruction, regrouping, over-pursuit,
irregular frontage, congestion, role viability, and deterministic repetition.
A mass scenario validates behavior and performance, not historical doctrine.

## Provisional candidate ranges

Every number below is **Provisional reconstruction:** gameplay tuning with no
historical measurement.

| Parameter | Range | Candidate default |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Unobstructed top-speed multiplier | 0.94–1.00 | 0.97 |
| Acceleration multiplier | 0.88–0.98 | 0.93 |
| Maximum facing-change multiplier | 0.80–0.95 | 0.88 |
| Lateral-travel multiplier | 0.88–1.00 | 0.94 |
| Backward-travel multiplier | 0.80–0.96 | 0.88 |
| Added ordinary-ally clearance | 0.10–0.35 body diameters | 0.20 |
| Added shield-peer clearance | 0.10–0.35 body diameters | 0.20 |
| Immediate neighborhood | 2–3 body diameters | 2.5 |
| Local-support neighborhood | 5–8 body diameters | 6 |

The first implementation uses the same added-clearance envelope for every ally
and then takes the larger of the two resolved profiles; it does not add a
shield-peer-only bonus. Weapon offsets must remain smaller than the shared
shield effect:

| Parameter | Kalis + shield | Itak + shield |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Preferred-distance offset from paired weapon reach | +0.10 to +0.30 reach | -0.10 to +0.10 reach |
| Lateral multiplier offset | -0.03 to 0.00 | 0.00 to +0.03 |
| Facing-change offset | no extra discrete step | no extra discrete step; retain faster lateral/pace adjustment |

Candidate ratio hysteresis: enter disengagement around ally:enemy
`0.67–0.80`, leave near `0.85–1.00`; consider advance near `1.25–1.50` only
while a free lane persists. These ratios are experimental bands, not facts.

**Note added 2026-07-31 — the shipped thresholds depart from the band above.**
The paragraph above is left exactly as this research proposed it. This note
records only that gameplay tuning chose different numbers; it does not record
that the research was wrong. The shipped disengagement entry threshold for the
Kalis-and-shield row is 17,500 basis points, which is 1.75 enemies per ally, or
an ally-to-enemy ratio of 0.571, and that sits below the proposed `0.67–0.80`
entry band. The shipped entry threshold for the Itak-and-shield row is 15,000
basis points, which is 1.50 enemies per ally, or a ratio of 0.667, and that
sits exactly at the bottom edge of the same band. Both rows release from
disengagement at 11,000 basis points, which is 1.10 enemies per ally, or a
ratio of 0.909, and that value does sit inside the proposed `0.85–1.00` release
band. Decision D4 of
[`2026-07-31-movement-v7-calibration-decisions.md`](../../archives/2026-08-06/movement/2026-07-31-movement-v7-calibration-decisions.md)
ratified this departure on 2026-07-31 as deliberate rather than accidental:
both shield rows tolerating more pressure before they disengage is the intended
"protected deliberation" reading of the shield, and that reading is now signed
off and owned rather than sitting unattributed. Every number in this note, like
every number in the tables above it, is a **Provisional reconstruction:**
gameplay tuning under the historical accuracy policy in `CLAUDE.md` section 7.
None of them is a historical measurement and none may be presented as one.

Calibration must compare shielded and shieldless counterparts with identical
seed and geometry. Reject values that create artificial speed, endless
backward kiting, threshold oscillation, universal shield dominance, rigid
lines, or nondeterministic movement.

## Rejected extrapolations and gaps

Do not derive from this research:

- shield walls, fixed ranks, universal handedness, or universal crouch;
- directional interception, shield bash, shove, trap, or strike;
- morale, panic, rout, surrender, fatigue, terrain, or pathfinding;
- exact historical 1v1, 2v2, or mass tactics; or
- adding shielded units to the shieldless V3 roster after the approved
  default-preset switch.

Open work: verify Morga and Scott at page level; inspect early vocabularies;
record the exact Boxer Codex folio; locate securely dated object measurements;
and test candidate values. No current source distinguishes historical
Kalis-and-shield from Itak-and-shield footwork.
