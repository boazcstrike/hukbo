# Itak — Work Blade: Movement Research PRD

## 1. Purpose and scope

This document defines the evidence boundary and movement hypotheses for
Hukbo's `WeaponId.Itak`. It covers both valid implemented loadouts:

- Itak + light organic armor + no shield; and
- Itak + light organic armor + Tall Hardwood Shield.

The scope is the full movement lifecycle: formation placement, approach,
spacing, target engagement, attack commitment, recovery, disengagement,
regrouping, pursuit, and tactical retreat. It treats individual movement and
contingent posture separately and accounts for local ally/enemy counts,
complete loadout composition, and surviving battlefield totals.

The live source currently defaults to V2, whose roster includes solo and
shielded Itak. The approved planning target will switch that default to the
already-registered V3 roster, which contains solo Itak only, without changing
either preset. Shielded Itak remains available through an explicitly selected
V2 scenario.

Evidence labels:

- **Documented:** directly described or pictured in a sixteenth-century
  source.
- **Documented, form uncertain:** the class, name, or broad affordance is
  attested, but the depicted-period form is uncertain.
- **Provisional reconstruction:** a reasoned movement or product hypothesis,
  not a recovered historical technique.

## 2. Executive finding

**Provisional reconstruction:** `Itak — Work Blade` is Hukbo's least secure
period identity. *Itak* is a Tagalog term for a large cutting implement or
bolo-like work blade, but the repository's prior research could not verify the
claimed 1613 vocabulary entry at source. The name therefore must not be
presented as proof that one standardized Itak form or fighting method existed
throughout the sixteenth-century archipelago.

**Documented, form uncertain:** The National Museum of the Philippines
describes bolo-class blades as practical household, farming, hunting, food
preparation, and ritual tools that were also used as weapons in later raiding
and resistance contexts. A 1920s Tagalog/Luzon museum record labels a scabbard
for an *Itak*. These later sources establish a later-documented
tool-and-weapon association, not a 1500s combat profile.

**Provisional reconstruction:** A compact, one-handed cutting blade must close
more deeply than Kalis to act, rewards short changes of line and rapid
reassessment, and becomes vulnerable if it remains at the end of a longer
weapon's reach. Solo Itak should therefore be the roster's most mobile
short-blade mover without exceeding the shared human top speed: it seeks
isolated or distracted threats, enters decisively, resets quickly, and
disengages early from divided threat angles. Itak + Tall Hardwood trades much
of that lateral and reverse freedom for a deliberate shielded close approach.

No source establishes triangular footwork, a universal checking hand, named
entries, combinations, shield angles, formal duel rules, or direct continuity
between modern FMA and sixteenth-century Itak practice.

### 3.2 Mechanical interpretation

**Provisional reconstruction:** Compared with Kalis, Hukbo's Itak has a shorter
effective weapon distance and a work-blade identity. Its movement should be
distinguished by decisions, not supernatural quickness:

- retain the shared human top speed rather than receive a weapon speed bonus;
- use relatively small forward and lateral constraints when shieldless;
- close only when an approach lane is viable;
- avoid prolonged exposure at the edge of a longer weapon's reach;
- limit continued forward movement during a committed cut;
- recover direction sooner than the heavier two-handed weapons; and
- prefer a nearby ally, flank, or distracted enemy over an unsupported frontal
  contest.

A broad or forward-weighted blade can improve cutting authority while raising
rotational inertia. That supports a visible commitment/recovery interval. The
available sources do not provide a measured center of mass, moment of inertia,
step length, turn speed, or cadence for a sixteenth-century Itak.

### 3.3 Modern combat-community comparison

**Provisional reconstruction:** FMA Pulse describes a modern bolo form that
widens and curves toward the tip, placing more weight near the end for
chopping, and notes that Tagalogs may call it *itak*. This is a bounded
modern-practitioner analogy, not evidence of sixteenth-century form or
practice.

The cited community description and modern FMA comparison material raise
questions about distance, angles, entry, exit, and recovery. Those concepts
are useful simulation prompts because a short live blade cannot act from every
distance. They do not demonstrate an unchanged ancient curriculum. Modern
stick sparring, protective equipment, one-on-one training conventions, and
postcolonial system names may change the movement problem substantially.

## 4. Full movement lifecycle

Every behavior in this section is **Provisional reconstruction**.

### 4.1 Formation placement

- Solo Itak begins within an irregular contingent cluster, not automatically
  on the front edge where longer weapons control contact.
- It needs a usable approach lane and enough side space to avoid colliding
  with a companion during entry or recovery.
- Shielded Itak may receive a roomier existing slot, but is not assigned to a
  rigid shield wall, rank, file, or bodyguard ring.
- Both variants retain a path toward a nearby ally, contingent leader, or
  regrouping direction.

### 4.2 Approach

- Solo Itak does not walk straight through a longer weapon's preferred
  distance. It waits for a locally favorable line, distracted target, ally
  pressure, or committed opposing action.
- Once the entry is viable, it closes decisively at the normal human movement
  budget; favorable numbers reduce hesitation, never increase speed.
- Shielded Itak uses shorter steps and prefers a distinct direct or
  shallow-oblique approach lane that does not demand a sudden large turn.
- Both variants slow before crowded contact so collision resolution does not
  become their only spacing system.

### 4.3 Spacing and target engagement

- Solo Itak wants to move through the danger band of a longer weapon rather
  than remain there. If it cannot close or leave, it seeks support.
- Against another short blade it preserves enough space to reverse or turn;
  “short range” does not mean permanent body-to-body crowding.
- Shielded Itak accepts closer measure but must protect an exit lane from
  opponents arriving on another bearing; movement grants no defensive cover.
- A second perceived enemy entering from a separated angle immediately raises
  disengagement priority over continuing a nominal 1v1.

### 4.4 Commitment, recovery, and reset

- An attack commitment permits only reduced locomotion and prevents an instant
  full-speed reversal.
- Solo Itak has a short recovery relative to heavier weapons, but recovery is
  not zero; otherwise the unit can oscillate or orbit unrealistically.
- Shielded Itak recovers turning and lateral movement more slowly because the
  carried shield constrains the whole-body change of line.
- Facing affects locomotion only. It does not alter attack arcs, damage,
  interception, hit location, combinations, or target validity.
- A reset is a movement state, not a claim about a documented Tagalog guard.

### 4.5 Disengagement, regrouping, pursuit, and retreat

- Solo Itak disengages earlier than Kalis when it cannot cross the opponent's
  reach safely or when nearby enemies can split its attention.
- Shielded Itak can yield more deliberately under mild pressure, but must not
  treat shield presence as immunity to encirclement.
- Both variants regroup toward locally perceived allies under their
  contingent's broader posture.
- Pursuit stops when it would isolate the Itak user, cross a withdrawal
  posture, or enter another enemy's uncontested range.
- Tactical retreat is repositioning, refusal, or regrouping. It is not morale,
  panic, rout, surrender, or battlefield flight.

## 5. Six-loadout 1v1 movement hypotheses

One-versus-one is a temporary local geometry, not a formal duel. The table
does not promise equal win rates. Every row is **Provisional reconstruction**.

| Opposing loadout | Solo Itak movement | Itak + Tall Hardwood movement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kampilan, no shield | Stay out until a committed cut creates an entry; close on a shallow angle, then reset or leave before recovery completes. | Advance in small shielded-loadout steps, avoid a large turn inside reach, and abandon a blocked entry. |
| Wasay, no shield | Avoid the planted chopping line, enter after commitment, and do not trade while stationary. | Narrow the approach lane through spacing, close deliberately, then restore exit space after the attempt. |
| Kalis, no shield | Do not linger at Kalis measure; either cross the distance with a viable line or disengage toward support. | Close through a free lane while denying free circling; keep an exit rather than pursuing a rotating target. |
| Kalis + Tall Hardwood | Refuse repeated frontal entry; use superior solo lateral freedom to change line, wait for ally pressure, or leave. | Expect a range disadvantage; advance only when local posture supports it and avoid a slow circling deadlock. |
| Itak, no shield | Keep enough room for entry and exit, use local context to break symmetry, and reset after each commitment. | Deny a free circling route through spacing; do not chase away from allies. |
| Itak + Tall Hardwood | Use solo mobility to seek a distinct collision-safe lane but disengage if local support is lost. | Mirror case: short probes, stable tie-breaking, and frequent reassessment rather than permanent face-to-face pressure. |

## 6. Count, composition, and group behavior

### 6.1 Knowledge boundary

- Individual Itak movement reads only perceived nearby allies, enemies, and
  their complete loadouts.
- Contingent or faction posture may use global surviving totals and
  composition for broad advance, cohesion, or withdrawal.
- Global posture never supplies exact remote positions to an individual.
- Runtime logic should use ratios, density, and a few stable thresholds.
  Named scenarios below are research and acceptance cases, not separate
  hard-coded modes.

### 6.2 Count scenarios

All responses are **Provisional reconstruction**.

| Local context | Solo Itak | Itak + Tall Hardwood |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1v1 | Seek a viable entry, commit briefly, reset, and abandon a bad range rather than orbit forever. | Close with shorter shielded-loadout steps and preserve the line of retreat. |
| 2v2 | Avoid entering through the ally's weapon lane; prefer a target already constrained by the ally. | Supply deliberate local pressure while leaving the partner a separate lane and exit. |
| 1v2 | Disengage early, before separated enemy angles form; move toward a perceived ally. | Yield through a free lane before enemies occupy separated bearings. |
| 2v3 | Maintain mutual support and refuse isolated pursuit; change target when the ally is screened off. | Stabilize the local withdrawal direction without becoming stationary. |
| 3v5 | Follow contingent regroup/withdraw posture, preserve an escape lane, and avoid unsupported deep entry. | Support local movement only while the group can still withdraw; no last-stand bonus. |
| Local advantage | Reduce hesitation and occupy escape space through positioning; never gain movement budget. | Advance slowly enough that allies can use different lanes instead of stacking behind the shield. |
| Large battle | React to local density and contingent posture, not exact distant positions. | Act as a deliberate local participant, not a permanent front-rank class. |

### 6.3 Composition effects

- A nearby Kampilan or Wasay ally needs swing room. Itak should approach from a
  different available line rather than crowd the ally.
- A nearby Kalis ally can hold a longer measure while Itak waits for an entry;
  this is emergent cooperation, not a scripted combo.
- A nearby shielded ally may offer a locally safer regrouping direction, but
  it provides no defense aura and does not justify clustering.
- A local enemy group rich in longer weapons raises the value of waiting,
  flank access, and allied pressure.
- A local enemy group rich in shields reduces the value of unsupported frontal
  entry and should trigger earlier regrouping.

## 7. Candidate movement ranges and default hypotheses

These are **Provisional reconstruction:** gameplay calibration hypotheses, not
historical measurements. Each factor multiplies the shared human movement budget.
Numerical advantage changes decisions only. “Reach” is the configured gameplay
reach, not a museum dimension.

| Lateral movement factor | 0.86–0.98 | 0.93 | 0.82–0.92 | 0.87 |
| Backward movement factor | 0.72–0.88 | 0.81 | 0.64–0.78 | 0.71 |

| Recovery duration | 1–3 ticks | 2 | 2–4 ticks | 3 |

| Pursuit support limit | 0.75–1.25 local ally spacing radii | 1.00 | 0.60–1.00 | 0.80 |

Candidate turning policy:

- solo Itak retains most movement on changes up to one-eighth turn, uses a
  reduced step between one-eighth and one-quarter turn, and takes more than one
  tick for larger reversals;
- shielded Itak uses the same bands with a larger reduction; and
- no variant can flip facing and move at full speed in the opposite direction
  on the same tick.

The implementation plan must choose exact integer/fixed-point representation,
hash order, snapshot fields, threshold equality, and preset versioning. None
of these candidates should enter code before oracle tests and calibration.
The shared Tall Hardwood research table is the canonical shield-delta
envelope; the shielded column above is its once-authored, rounded absolute
materialization for Itak and is never multiplied again at runtime.

## 8. Calibration questions and acceptance evidence

1. Can solo Itak cross Kalis, Wasay, or Kampilan danger distance sometimes
   without becoming either helpless or a permanent orbiting kiter?
2. Does its short recovery remain visibly nonzero?
3. Does shielded Itak gain a deliberate approach rhythm while losing enough
   lateral and reverse freedom to remain distinct?
4. In 1v2, do both variants disengage before encirclement without receiving a
   speed boost?
5. In 2v2, does Itak exploit ally pressure without occupying the same lane?
6. Does each variant contribute in at least one individual and one group
   context without requiring equal duel win rates?
7. At exact count and distance thresholds, are decisions stable across
   repeated runs and incidental collection order?
8. At 100v100 and 250v250, do local-count and composition decisions remain
   bounded and avoid a new full-agent scan for every factor?
9. When the new movement preset is not selected, do all frozen movement
   presets retain their replay and digest results?

Record approach distance, time exposed outside own reach, commitment/recovery
time, successful and failed entries, disengagement attempts, isolation time,
ally spacing, blocked movement, state changes, winner, event hash, and state
hash. Manual spectator review remains separate evidence.

## 10. Unknowns and non-goals

Unknown:

- whether `itak` was the correct name for this combat role in the early 1500s;
- sixteenth-century form, length, mass, balance, point, guard, grip, and
  regional distribution;
- normal pairing with a shield;
- handedness, stance, lead foot, step sequence, turn speed, commitment,
  recovery, feints, combinations, or target priorities;
- specific 1v1, 2v2, or mass-battle doctrine;
- historical movement-speed or numerical-disadvantage thresholds; and
- continuity between modern FMA/bolo practice and sixteenth-century warfare.

This PRD does not authorize:

- damage, reach, cooldown, combo, clash, shield-interception, or hit-location
  rebalance;
- directional shield defense or weapon attack arcs;
- rigid ranks, shield walls, formal duel rules, morale, panic, surrender, or
  rout;
- terrain, fatigue, injuries, pathfinding, projectiles, campaign systems, or
  a default-roster change;
- treating all bolo-class objects as one standardized Itak; or
- describing modern triangular footwork or named FMA techniques as unchanged
  precolonial survival.

The defensible product claim is narrow: Itak movement is a transparent,
deterministic gameplay reconstruction based on the affordances of a later
documented Philippine work-blade family, general period evidence for mobile
group combat, and explicitly limited modern handling analogies.
